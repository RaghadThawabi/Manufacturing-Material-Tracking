Waste analysis in a cutting job 
The situation. A printed-carton job uses more board than expected. The production team wants to distinguish true waste from 
reusable offcuts and see whether the loss is associated with setup, layout or damaged material. 
Why this matters. The factory issued 1,200 kg of board to one job. The team needs to know how much became saleable cartons, 
how much was lost and how much can be reused. Mixing reusable pieces with waste would make the problem look larger than it 
is. 
Who would use it. The cutting operator, production supervisor and manager. 
Proposed internship tool. A digital job card where the operator records the board issued and the weight of good product, waste 
by reason and reusable pieces. The tool checks that these figures add back to the issued weight, then compares this job with 
another job on the same machine. 
Terms used here. Scrap is material treated as waste in this exercise. Offcuts are leftover pieces that can be reused. Yield is good 
output divided by material issued. 
Case records and assumptions 
Case input 
Case record 
Job IK-202 
1,200 kg board issued → 900 kg good cut product + 180 kg setup/layout scrap + 60 kg 
damaged scrap + 60 kg reusable offcuts 
Comparison job IK-203 
Machine 
1,000 kg board issued → 820 kg good product + 100 kg scrap + 80 kg reusable offcuts 
Cutting machine CT-2 for both jobs 
Specification 
Product, sheet size, planned yield and cutting instruction attached to each job 
How staff would use the tool 
1.  Operator opens a job card, selects material and cutting machine, and records issued weight. 
2.  At job close, staff record good output, each scrap reason, reusable offcuts and any rework separately. 
3.  The application rejects an unbalanced job and displays yield and waste with explicit definitions. 
4.  Manager compares IK-202 with IK-203 and opens the records behind the difference. 
What the tool should show 
• IK-202 balances at 1,200 kg: 900 good + 240 scrap + 60 reusable. Scrap rate = 240 ÷ 1,200 = 20%; good yield = 75%. 
• IK-203 scrap rate = 100 ÷ 1,000 = 10%. IK-202 is 10 percentage points higher. 
• The largest loss category for IK-202 is setup/layout scrap at 180 kg. 
Questions it should answer. Where did the 1,200 kg go? Which waste category is largest, and how does this job compare with 
the other job?

A product that could serve other factories 
Proposed product. Cutting material yield tool 
Core promise. Show where board or paper goes in every cutting job and identify the biggest avoidable material losses without 
counting reusable pieces as waste. 
Potential customers beyond the pilot factory. Carton producers, commercial printers, label producers and other converters 
that cut sheets and record material use by job.
